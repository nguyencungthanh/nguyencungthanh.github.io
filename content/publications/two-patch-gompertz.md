## Abstract

This paper investigates whether the Maximum Sustainable Yield (MSY) can be enhanced in multi-patch fishery systems when fish populations follow Gompertz rather than logistic growth. We present a
two-patch fishery model with patch-dependent catchability, Schaefer-type
harvesting and fish migration of arbitrary speed. We analyze how spatial structure, growth formulation, heterogeneous harvesting, and environmental variability influence the MSY. Analytical expressions for the global MSY are derived in the limiting case of infinitely fast migration and compared to the sum of local MSYs of isolated patches. Our results show that, as in the logistic case, the total MSY cannot exceed the sum of the local MSYs and that equality occurs when fish distribute across patches according to an ideal free distribution generalized to the Gompertz framework. Building on this result, we investigate how spatial management can be used to minimize the fishing effort required to achieve MSY. In particular, we demonstrate that appropriately designed Marine Protected Area configurations, combined with spatial heterogeneity in catchability, can redistribute biomass in a way that reduces effort while maintaining optimal yield. The framework is further extended to a prey–predator system with Gompertz prey growth and predator harvesting, where spatial heterogeneity and asymmetric migration can enhance the global MSY, highlighting the role of trophic interactions in shaping sustainable exploitation. Finally, we assess the robustness of these results under environmental stochasticity. We show that when stochastic perturbations are sufficiently small, the qualitative and quantitative properties derived from the deterministic model, particularly those concerning MSY and equilibrium structure, are preserved. This robustness analysis strengthens the applicability of the proposed framework for real-world fisheries, where environmental variability is unavoidable.
